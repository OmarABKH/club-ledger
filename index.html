Method

How this is put together

  • Clubs. The clubs in each league in the 2026/27 season, with their finances from the three most recent seasons, whichever league they were in at the time.
  • Source. Each club's annual accounts filed at Companies House (Bolton's are published by the club for its parent company). 2024/25 comes from the accounts for that year; 2023/24 and 2022/23 come from the 2023/24 accounts, which show the previous year alongside. Figures are as reported, in pounds.
  • Restated figures. Clubs sometimes correct a previous year's figures in the following year's accounts. The site shows each year as first reported (Cardiff's 2023/24 staff costs, for example, were later restated), and notes where this happens.
  • Which company. The club's main operating company, or the group that owns it where that's where the full accounts are filed (for example Arsenal Holdings, Chelsea FC Holdings, and NSWE UK for Aston Villa, whose club company doesn't employ the players). The company used is named on each club's page.
  • Staff costs. Wages, bonuses, social security and pensions from each club's staff costs note, including redundancy and termination payments that some clubs show separately as exceptional items.
  • Different year ends. Most clubs' years end on 30 June; some use 31 May or 31 July. A few periods are longer or shorter because a club changed its year end (Aston Villa 2023/24, Wolves 2024/25 and Bristol City 2022/23: 13 months; Swansea 2023/24: 11 months). These are marked in the table and on club pages.
  • Revenue categories. Clubs split revenue differently. Their lines are mapped to matchday, broadcasting and commercial & other; anything that doesn't fit cleanly (e.g. catering) goes into commercial & other.
  • Checking the numbers. The filings are scanned images, so they're read with OCR (text recognition). Every revenue breakdown is checked to add up to the reported total, and staff costs and the result before tax are checked against the income statement.
  • Not a spending-rules (PSR) calculation. Clubs can exclude spending on academies, women's football, community work and infrastructure from the Premier League's and EFL's profit and sustainability limits, and those figures aren't fully disclosed. Some clubs have also booked large one-off gains from selling assets to companies under the same owners; these are flagged on the club's page. A large loss here doesn't by itself mean a club broke the rules.
